## Late game

Once players reach the late stages of the game, they should have built an empire with a considerably strong fleet and a strong production of resources. By this stage, players should be pursuing one of the many victory goals present in the game by default. However, the late stage of the game usually involves events that affect the entire galaxy. Note that Late Game has both a general meaning as well as a specific mechanical meaning as the game setup has an End-Game Start Year setting which acts as a trigger for certain events or other mechanics.

While Stellaris is primarily a sandbox game without a specific win-condition, when the game reaches the Victory Year, the empire with the highest score is declared the "winner"; however, the game can be continued after this point with no changes in gameplay.

### Megastructures

Main article: Megastructures

Megastructures are colossal constructions that require massive amounts of resources and long periods of time to build. The benefits of these structures should not be dismissed, however, as they are equally massive. A few megastructures can be constructed earlier in the game, such as Habitats, however most megastructures cannot even be researched until the Mega-Engineering technology has been researched, and require tens of thousands of alloys to bring to completion. Other than Gateways, all megastructures are unlocked by DLC; which DLC is required can be found on the Megastructures page.

### Endgame crisis

Main article: Crisis

The endgame crisis is, in some ways, the climax of the game, a seemingly unstoppable force that threatens to destroy the entire galaxy. The endgame crisis cannot appear before the End-Game Start Year outside of certain event choices, and normally appears around 50 years after that year. There are four possible endgame crises:

- The Contingency
- Extradimensional Invaders
- Prethoryn Scourge
- The Synthetic Queen

Which crisis appears can be determined during game setup – it is random by default – and its strength can also be increased or decreased. It is also possible to have all crisis factions appear one after another, each being double the strength of the last. Defeating the endgame crisis typically requires a very powerful empire or coalition of empires. For more details and strategies, refer to the Crisis page.

An empire can similarly pursue the Cosmogenesis route by selecting the ascension perk of that name, developing technology on par with the fallen empires and eventually leaving the galaxy to travel to another universe, causing the deaths of untold amounts of people through the Synaptic Lathe and inflicting immense damage to the galaxy in the process.

### Awakened empire

Main article: Fallen empire#Awakened Empires

In certain situations, a fallen empire may Awaken and begin expanding their influence over the galaxy once again, in particular seeking to force other empires to be their subjects. Defeating an awakened empire can yield certain otherwise unobtainable technologies and some of their homeworlds contain special buildings, which cannot normally be constructed, making them valuable targets for conquest.

#### War in Heaven

|  | Available only with the Leviathans DLC enabled. |
|---|---|

Main article: War in Heaven

In rare cases, two Fallen Empires may awaken and launch a great war against one another, with the rest of the galaxy prompted to join a side or stay neutral. The empires which remain neutral may also form a special federation, the League of Non-Aligned Powers.

### Ascension perks

Main article: Ascension perk

After completing a tradition tree, the player can unlock ascension perks; these range from simple, but powerful, bonuses to unlocking new mechanics such as:

- One Vision gives −10% pop amenities usage, +10% monthly unity, +50% governing ethics attraction
- Colossus Project unlocks the construction of Colossi – world destroyer ships, and owning a Colossus adds a total war casus belli against all empires.
- Galactic Wonders unlocks certain megastructures – depending on DLC: Ring Worlds and Dyson Spheres with Utopia, Matter Decompressors with MegaCorp
- World Shaper, Arcology Project, Hive Worlds, and Machine Worlds enable terraforming planets to gaia worlds, ecumenopolises, hive worlds, and machine worlds, respectively.

#### Ascension paths

|  | Available only with the Utopia DLC enabled. |
|---|---|

Main article: Ascension path

The player can pursue four mutually-exclusive ascension paths:

- Genetics
- Psionics
- Cybernetics
- Synthetics

Each of these represents, in one way or another, the ultimate evolution of the empire’s species. Taking the ascension path perk unlocks an ascension path tradition, and so requires having less than seven tradition trees already adopted.

- Genetic ascension unlocks new species traits and modification options.
- Psionic ascension enables contacting the Shroud, including the possibility to form a powerful covenant.
- Cybernetic ascension enables adding cybernetic traits to organic species.
- Synthetic ascension improves robots and unlocks a project to turn all organic pops into robots.

|  | Available only with the The Machine Age DLC enabled. |
|---|---|

Synthetic ascension for Machine Intelligence empires are further divided into three distinct paths.

- Modularity ascension focuses on Living Metal for species modification and improving machine pops.
- Nanotech ascension focuses on Nanites for building unique types of ships.
- Virtuality ascension focuses on improving population efficiency across a small number of colonies. It is not recommended for beginners as it drastically changes the conventional play style of constant expansion across the entire galaxy, and will require more strategic considerations.

##### The End of the Cycle

Main article: The Reckoning

Once an empire has completed the Psionic Ascension Path, interacting with the Shroud can give the opportunity to sign a covenant with the End of the Cycle in the Shroud. This is very rare, with only a 2% chance of occurring, but it gives huge boosts to resource production, research speed, and naval capacity for 50 years. At the end of this period, the reckoning occurs. The player loses all their planets and they become uninhabitable forever. All ships are destroyed and all pops and leaders are killed. The player gains control of a single new planet called Exile with a group of surviving pops and an entity called the "Reckoning" spawns, with a fleet power measuring in the millions. It attempts to kill everything in the galaxy saving the players for last. All empires will also get −1000 opinion of the player for "Bringing the End".

## General Tips

- Early exploration is key to establishing your empire. Investing in one or two extra science ships in the early game will help to accelerate that exploration. However, note that this will delay unlocking traditions, as you must spend unity to hire new scientists.
- The galaxy is a dangerous place. It is important to maintain a strong fleet to discourage your neighbors from declaring an opportunistic war. A strong fleet will also allow you to clear out any space creatures in systems you want to explore or expand to, as well as deal with pirates that might appear.
  - Keep in mind the positioning of your military too. If your fleets are all on one side of your empire, it might take years for them to cross it to meet a threat at the other side, and by that time the threat could have occupied up a huge portion of your empire. Starbases alone cannot defend against enemy fleets. This increases the reason to consider having multiple fleets to defend all sides of your empire.
  - Making defensive pacts or a federation with your neighbors will also help to protect against an unexpected attack, both by dissuading the attacker from even declaring and by adding additional fleets to fight against your enemy.
- Don't worry about maximizing every planet and action. Specialized planets and ship designs do provide an edge, but are not necessary to build a strong and fun empire.

- Remain curious and have fun. As your science ships discover anomalies, take the time to read the flavor, not just the effects. Stellaris contains many stories, and you will never encounter them all in a single game.

## The Art of War: Some general advice and tips

Be advised! This section is a bit more complex than everything featured above. Make sure you have a good enough grasp of the basics of Stellaris before proceeding onward.

There are a multitude of ways to wage war in Stellaris, but for players who are unsure on how to go about it, here are some words of advice to help establish a foundation of elementary knowledge.

### Your Empire

- One should always make sure to have the basic fundamentals in order. A strong backbone for one's military makes everything else easier. Fully completing the Supremacy and Unyielding and Enmity Tradition trees and picking the associated Galactic Force Projection and Eternal Vigilance Ascension perks will help with militarizing your empire to confront any threats you may encounter. Ethic-civic combinations like Fanatic Militarist and Distinguished Admiralty, which both add up to a +30% Ship Fire Rate bonus, also helps noticeably with waging war. It may seem simple, but every little bit helps.
  - On the home front, civics like Mining Guilds and Relentless Industrialists (particularly on a Forge World Ecumenopolis) will help with building up a solid economic base, allowing you to field larger fleets, build more starbase defenses and more easily and swiftly replace any losses. Likewise, building Megastructures (such as a Dyson Sphere, a Matter Decompressor and Arc Furnaces) and Ecumenopolis worlds will further contribute to enhancing your economic, and by extension, military capabilities.

  - Combining the Nationalistic Zeal and Warrior Culture civics with the Interstellar Campaigns and Galactic Campaigns technologies will give you a bonus of −50% war exhaustion, which allows you to sustain wars for longer and withstand greater losses, potentially allowing you to outlast your opponents and effectively win by endurance.
  - Fortifying your systems by building defense platforms around your upgraded Starbases and constructing Deep Space Citadels and Orbital Rings with defense modules around dedicated Fortress worlds will make your systems that much harder to capture, making attacks against you all the more costly for enemies.
  - Once you research and acquire it, the Existential Campaigns technology allows you to declare a Total War against other empires (like a Genocidal empire), allowing you to destroy starbases outright and seize ownership of planets through occupation alone, disregarding claims altogether. This allows you to completely destroy an opposing empire and seize their territory for yourself quickly and brutally efficiently.

  - If you wish to ratchet up your militarization even further, albeit at the cost of effectively destroying all diplomatic relations with the rest of the galaxy, then you can opt to select the Galactic Nemesis Ascension Perk as your 4th ascension perk, which will allow you to build special Menacing ships (which are built directly from minerals and both their build and upkeep costs are fixed, allowing you to mass-produce them in staggering quantities) and gain a number of very powerful empire-wide stat bonuses. Be aware, however, that doing this will require a considerable amount of preparatory groundwork (the collection of menace) to become fully effective, and said groundwork, due to its nature, comes with a considerable risk of making other empires hostile towards you.
    - BEWARE! Reaching Crisis Level 5 will instantly cause the Galactic Community to declare your empire a crisis and declare war, and Fallen Empires can now even awaken as Guardians of the Galaxy against you! This is therefore not recommended unless you really know what you are doing and are confident in your ability to handle it.

- Selecting the Enigmatic Engineering ascension perk as your second perk will allow you to draw up to 4 Fallen empire building technologies (and their upgrades) as research options, giving you an early economic edge. Be warned, however, that each fallen empire technology researched gives a stacking −10 opinion penalty with all existing fallen empires (doubled to −20 with the Keepers of Knowledge), which increases the chances of them becoming hostile toward you, so beware.
  - Continuing down on this path; selecting the Cosmogenesis Ascension perk as your 4th ascension perk, will allow you to start down on the path towards unlocking and utilizing all of the fallen empires' technologies yourself, allowing you to eventually reach the same level of power as they have and beyond. As mentioned earlier, always beware the stacking opinion penalty, as this ascension perk will cause it to eventually climb high enough to make the fallen empires issue demands to you, which if refused, will lead to war.

- Diplomacy can also be leveraged as a weapon in its own right, as having allies to fight with you is a welcome thing in any war. The ethic-civic combination of Fanatic Xenophile and Diplomatic Corps alongside fully completing the Diplomacy and Politics Tradition trees (and optionally Subterfuge) will allow you to form federations to ally with other empires as well as gain enough envoys to both hold the federation together and to forge ties with other empires to either bring them into the federation as members or at least make them associates to prevent war with them. Forming a federation allows you to not only have allies to fight alongside but also enables you to build and field a Federation fleet in addition to your own fleet(s), which is a very useful asset to have in war.

  - As for federation types, the Galactic Union is unlocked by default and works in a pinch (and is easier to hold together) while the Martial Alliance is unlocked by the Unyielding tradition tree and is more militarily capable. If you complete the Domination tradition tree, you will be able to form a Hegemony, which prevents members from freely leaving the federation, ensuring that everyone stays together when needed. Making other empires into subjects (particularly specialized ones) will further strengthen your grip and control over your allies. The Feudal Society or Franchising civics, the Shared Destiny Ascension Perk and the Intergalactic Directives resolutions will help you with retaining subjects.

  - In addition to federations, the aforementioned ethic-civic-tradition combination will also allow you to work towards gaining membership in the Galactic Council and then becoming the Galactic Custodian, the latter of which will allow you to build and field a Galactic Defense Fleet, which effectively gives you a second Federation fleet. You will also gain an increase to Diplomatic weight, allowing you to work on passing desirable Resolutions and repealing undesirable ones. For military matters, the best resolution categories would be the Industrial Development and Mutual Defense lines, and optionally the Galactic Commerce and Unchained Knowledge lines as well. If you are Galactic Custodian, then the Politics resolutions will also be useful. Building an Interstellar Assembly Megastructure will also provide both additional envoys, increase your diplomatic weight give an opinion bonus from all empires, which further helps with conducting diplomacy.

- When facing a Crisis, the easiest way to improve your fighting chances is to stack up damage bonuses against crises. The Defender of the Galaxy ascension perk (+50%), membership in a level 5 Galactic Union or Martial Alliance (+50% each), the Galactic Threats Committe politics resolution and the A United Front custodian reform (+20% each) will all give damage bonuses against crisis factions. If you acquire all of them, your ships can gain up to a +140% damage bonus against crisis factions, more than doubling their damage.

### Ships

- To field military ships, you need Naval Capacity, as exceeding it will increase ship upkeep. Sources of Naval Capacity include building Anchorages and Naval Logistics Offices on upgraded Starbases and Orbital rings, building Strongholds and Fortresses on planets to generate Soldier and Battle Thrall jobs.
  - Naval Capacity is not a hard cap, however. If your economy can handle it, then it is perfectly viable to exceed your current naval capacity in an emergency or for any number of reasons. AI empires will exceed their naval capacity if the situation requires it.
- Every ship has a build cost of Alloys (Mechanical ships) or Food (Organic ships) and a recurring logistical upkeep cost of Trade Value. Additionally, advanced components will also require Strategic Resources as upkeep. Make sure to have a reliable and adequate production capacity of each of these resources to ensure you can both build, maintain and replace military ships.
  - Having a powerful military-industrial apparatus and economy is vital for sustaining a modern military. Efficient planetary management, district and building construction and job provision along with a matching population goes hand in hand with having a capable military force.

- Military ships, their components, their related infrastructure and a multitude of other useful items are obtained through the research and development of Technology, so investing resources and effort into that will pay dividends over time. Even in real life, military R&D is one of the most well-funded and influential fields of science and its application, both in history and the modern day, and there is a reason why "tech-rushing", or focusing heavily on rapid acquisition of new technologies, is such an enduring strategy within the Stellaris community.
  - The most immediately relevant fields of research for military matters would be Field Manipulation, Particles, Military Theory, Materials, Propulsion and Voidcraft. Other fields do also contain many useful technologies, but these are the most directly influential ones.
  - Whenever a better version of an existing military technology is available to research, you should always select it for development and once available for use, you should always make use of it, to stay ahead or at least keep pace in the galactic arms race. If you are behind and need to catch up, then this becomes especially critical.

- Military ship hulls are made up of 1-3 hull sections, and each hull section can have a certain set of weapon and utility components installed on them. The variety of available hull section variants combined with the great number of different weapon and utility components available means that the number of possible combinations is immense, and each has its strengths, weaknesses and use cases or niches.
  - If you are uncertain of what to use where and when, the Auto-Design feature, given a ship role can help you generate some basic ship configurations to get started with. Auto-Design can also be given a ship role to generate more specialised designs to work with. From there, feel free to mix and match different sections and components together and experiment your way forward to figure out what works and what doesn't. There are also a variety of tutorial advisory videos available on YouTube to utilize as well, to gain further inspiration. and Just like in real life, there was a lot of trial and error-based experimentation involved in the designing of many weapon systems, and the constantly evolving and changing nature of war means that there is always more knowledge to be gained.

- AI empires will change, re-design and alter their ship configurations to adapt to changing circumstances and to counter any threats they face. Always be prepared to do the same, so as to not be outsmarted or be left behind.
- Military ships are grouped together into military fleets and each fleet can have a Commander assigned to lead it. Having a commander is not required, but it is always beneficial, as commanders provide various bonuses to their fleet both by themselves and through various leader traits.
- Always try to consolidate your fleets as much as possible and keep them together, particularly when heading into a battle, as spreading them out runs the risk of them being caught alone and picked off individually by superior forces. Some might call it "doom-stacking", but given that the AI also does this, and the alternative is risking defeat, one cannot argue with it.

### Armies

- In order to secure final victory against an opposing empire, you have to occupy their owned planets. This can be accomplished by either forcing a surrender through continued orbital bombardment until all enemy armies on the planet are destroyed, or by deploying Assault Armies to invade and secure the planet.
  - Since colonized planets (along with Starbases) can have FTL Inhibitors on them, there will be cases where you must secure and occupy an enemy planet to allow your fleet(s) to proceed further into enemy territory.
- Armies can be recruited both from upgraded Starbases and from colonized Planets. The number of armies of a given species that can be recruited is dependent on the number of pops that species has. You can recruit 1 army for every 100 pops in your empire. Each army comes with its own transport ship which can be grouped together into their own fleets and are used to carry the armies to their destination. Once in orbit of a planet, the armies can be ordered to land and commence assault.
  - When recruiting armies, be aware of what traits the species in question has, as this can affect the performance of each individual army. While it is possible to overcome stronger opposition with superior numbers, each army lost during an invasion gives war exhaustion, which can make sustaining loner campaigns harder, so it pays to be aware of this.

  - Like Military Fleets, Transport Fleets can have a Commander assigned to lead them, who will then command the armies during a planetary battle, providing different bonuses to the armies. This is usually of secondary importance compared to having commanders leading fleets, but it can be useful to have a dedicated general to lead armies when invading more heavily defended planets.
  - Army Transports are defenseless, so always make sure to keep them out of harm's way and do not let them be caught undefended and be destroyed by an enemy force, as the loss of these armies will cause war exhaustion for you.
- To defend against enemy invasions, you need to recruit Defense Armies and station them on your owned planets. This is accomplished by building Strongholds and Fortresses on planets to generate Soldier and Battle Thrall jobs, which spawn Defense Armies. Additionally, building Precinct Houses and Halls of Judgment will generate Enforcer jobs, which will also spawn some Defense Armies as well and if you have the Citizen Service and Sovereign Guardianship civics, you can also gain from Soldier jobs. Since Defense Armies can be damaged and destroyed by orbital bombardment, you should also build a Planetary Shield Generator if possible, as it will cause all bombardment damage to be cut in half, allowing armies on the planet to last longer under fire.

  - Once again, the species occupying the job that spawns the Defense Armies will affect what stats the armies will have, so be aware of this.
  - When bombarding a planet with defense armies on it, the situation is effectively comparable to a siege. Given enough time, the planet will be forced to surrender once all defending armies have been eliminated, but this takes time and gives your opponents time to, for instance, potentially prepare for and mount a counterattack. For this reason, it may sometimes be preferable to use Assault Armies to invade and quickly secure control of a planet, since waiting out the time needed to force a surrender through bombardment alone can be detrimental in some cases.
  - If there are no Defense Armies available on a planet and circumstances do not allow you to recruit any, then as a stopgap measure, you can also recruit Assault Armies and station them on said planet to defend it from invasions. This is not ideal, as Assault Armies receive double damage from orbital bombardment and will cause some collateral damage, but it can work adequately well in a pinch.
